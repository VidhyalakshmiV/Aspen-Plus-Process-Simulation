# Aspirin Production Using Vacuum Reactive Distillation

### Process Simulation and Optimization Using Aspen Plus

A chemical engineering project focused on the design and simulation of a **vacuum reactive distillation process** for aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) production, exploring the integration of chemical reaction and product separation into a single process unit.

---

## 📌 Project Overview

Aspirin, chemically known as acetylsalicylic acid, is widely used for its analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and cardiovascular benefits. The growing demand for high-purity pharmaceutical products creates a need for efficient, economical, and resource-conscious manufacturing processes.

Conventional aspirin production generally involves reaction and separation operations performed in separate process units. This project investigates **vacuum reactive distillation** as an alternative approach that integrates reaction and separation within a single column.

Using **Aspen Plus**, the project focuses on understanding process behavior, evaluating the influence of operating conditions, and exploring opportunities to improve aspirin production efficiency.

## 🎯 Project Objectives

* Design and simulate a vacuum reactive distillation column using Aspen Plus.
* Investigate aspirin synthesis from salicylic acid and acetic anhydride.
* Examine the effects of reactant composition ratios on process performance.
* Study the influence of temperature conditions across different sections of the column.
* Explore the role of vacuum operation in reaction and separation.
* Evaluate the potential for improved product yield, reduced equipment requirements, and lower energy consumption.

## ⚗️ Chemical Reaction

Aspirin is synthesized through the acetylation of salicylic acid using acetic anhydride in the presence of an acid catalyst.

**Reactants:**

* Salicylic acid — C₇H₆O₃
* Acetic anhydride — (CH₃CO)₂O

**Catalyst:**

* Sulfuric acid — H₂SO₄

**Products:**

* Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) — C₉H₈O₄
* Acetic acid — CH₃COOH

### Overall Reaction

```text
Salicylic Acid + Acetic Anhydride
              |
              |  H₂SO₄ catalyst
              v
     Acetylsalicylic Acid + Acetic Acid
             (Aspirin)
```

## 🔬 Process Concept

The proposed process combines reaction and separation in a vacuum reactive distillation column.

1. **Feed Introduction:** Salicylic acid and acetic anhydride are introduced into the process.
2. **Catalytic Reaction:** Sulfuric acid facilitates the acetylation reaction.
3. **Reactive Distillation:** Reaction and separation are integrated within the column.
4. **Vacuum Operation:** Reduced-pressure operation is investigated as a means of influencing vapor–liquid equilibrium and separation behavior.
5. **Process Evaluation:** Operating conditions and feed composition are examined to understand their effects on conversion, product recovery, and overall process performance.

*The actual performance of the process depends on the selected operating conditions, thermodynamic model, reaction kinetics, and column configuration.*

## 🌱 Expected Benefits

The project explores the potential of vacuum reactive distillation to:

* **Improve process integration:** Combine reaction and separation within a single unit.
* **Reduce equipment requirements:** Potentially decrease the need for separate processing equipment.
* **Optimize operating conditions:** Identify conditions that may improve process performance.
* **Support energy efficiency:** Investigate opportunities to reduce overall energy demand.
* **Enhance process economics:** Explore the feasibility of a more efficient aspirin manufacturing route.

These benefits are objectives for investigation rather than guaranteed outcomes; they must be established through simulation and appropriate validation.

## 🏭 Industrial Relevance

The project connects chemical reaction engineering, separation processes, thermodynamics, and process optimization in a pharmaceutical manufacturing context.

The underlying approach may also be relevant to other chemical processes where reaction and separation can be effectively integrated. However, industrial implementation would require further evaluation of product purity, process safety, catalyst compatibility, materials, economics, and pharmaceutical quality requirements.
